## 1. Mission

Our team is responsible for developing a Support Deflection MVP for Northstar Retail Co.

The purpose of the MVP is to demonstrate how a customer-facing self-service experience can reduce repetitive support requests.

Our frontend prototype focuses on:

- Order Status
- Returns & Refunds

The goal is to create a simple, clear and demoable customer experience within the five-day sprint.

## 3. Working Agreement

## 4. Task Management

Project tasks are managed through GitHub Projects.

Each task must have:

- An owner.
- A priority.
- A status.
- A Definition of Done.

No individual task should require more than four hours.

Tasks that are too large must be divided into smaller, clearly defined tasks.



## 5. Definition of Done

A task is considered **Done** when:

- The assigned work has been completed.
- The expected result has been achieved.
- The work has been tested where applicable.
- Known issues have been documented.
- The changes have been committed and pushed to GitHub.
- The project task has been updated.
- The completed work can be demonstrated to another team member.

**Started does not mean Done.**

A task is Done only when its agreed and checkable outcome has been achieved.



## 6. GitHub Workflow

Team members will use branches for their assigned development work.

The general workflow is:

1. Create or switch to the appropriate branch.
2. Complete the assigned task.
3. Test the work.
4. Commit the changes.
5. Push the branch to GitHub.
6. Create a Pull Request where required.
7. Review changes.
8. Merge approved work into the main branch.

### Commit Convention

The team will use:

`<type>: <what changed> - <why it matters>`

Example:

`feat: build order status page - gives customers a self-service order lookup interface`

Vague commit messages such as `update`, `changes`, or `wip` should be avoided.

---

## 7. Board Update Rule

Team members must update the project task status on the same day that work takes place.

The team will not wait until the end of the sprint to update completed work.

This keeps the project history accurate and traceable.



## 8. Blocker Rule

When a team member becomes blocked, they should:

1. Attempt to solve the problem.
2. Record what they have tried.
3. Inform the team.
4. Ask for assistance if necessary.
5. Escalate to the team lead when required.

The team will address blockers early rather than waiting until the deadline.



## 9. Two-Day Activity Rule

If a team member has zero visible activity for two or more days, the team will:

1. Contact the team member.
2. Determine whether they are blocked.
3. Offer assistance.
4. Reassign urgent work if necessary.
5. Escalate according to the team agreement.

The purpose is to protect the sprint and ensure that problems are addressed early.


## 10. Quality Agreement

Before delivery, the team will verify that:

- The required support categories are represented.
- The main customer journeys are complete.
- Navigation works correctly.
- Forms and buttons behave as intended.
- The interface is visually consistent.
- The prototype is responsive where required.
- Known limitations are documented.
- GitHub activity is available.
- Project tasks are up to date.
- Required documentation is complete.



## 11. Sprint Success Criteria

The sprint will be considered successful when:

- At least two support categories are represented.
- The frontend prototype is demoable.
- The customer journey is clear.
- The interface is usable.
- Team contributions are visible.
- Work can be traced through GitHub activity.
- Required documentation is complete.
- The MVP can be handed over for future development.



## 12. Team Commitment

All team members agree to communicate openly, contribute honestly, maintain visible project activity, raise blockers early and work together to deliver the Northstar Support Deflection MVP.
